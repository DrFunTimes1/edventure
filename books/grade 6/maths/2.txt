CHAPTER 2: LINES AND ANGLES (Short)

This chapter covers basic geometry: point, line, line segment, ray, and angle.

A point shows a location with no size (A, B, P).
A line segment is the shortest path between two points (AB) with fixed length.
A line extends infinitely in both directions.
A ray starts at one point and extends infinitely in one direction.

An angle is formed by two rays with a common vertex and shows rotation.

Types:
Acute < 90°, Right = 90°, Obtuse 90°–180°, Straight = 180°, Reflex 180°–360°.

Angles are measured in degrees. Full turn = 360°, half = 180°, quarter = 90°.

A protractor measures angles (180° semicircle). Place at vertex, align one arm to 0°, read other arm.

Drawing: draw arm → place protractor → mark degree → join vertex.

Angle bisector divides an angle into two equal parts.

Compare angles by measuring or superimposing; more rotation = larger angle.

Angles represent rotation, seen in doors, clocks, swings, wheels.

Key facts:
Point = location, segment = two points, line = infinite, ray = one direction, angle = rotation, not length.
